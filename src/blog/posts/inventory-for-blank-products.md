Custom stores sell blanks they do not print until an order arrives. That makes inventory easy to forget until a popular size runs out halfway through a sale.

## Track every variation

A hoodie in five sizes and four colours is twenty stock items, not one. Track each variation so you know exactly which ones run low.

## Stop before zero

Set a threshold where a variation stops selling, a few items above zero. That buffer covers misprints and damaged blanks.

## Watch the carts

During busy weeks, check how many items are sitting in carts. They are not sold yet, but they tell you what is about to sell.

## Reorder early

Reorder your bestsellers before the season starts. Suppliers run short at the same time you do.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting per product, not per variation.** You can have plenty of hoodies and still be out of the size everyone wants.
- **A threshold of zero.** By the time stock reaches zero, a misprint leaves you unable to fill an order you already accepted.
- **Updating stock by hand.** Manual counts drift. Let orders update stock automatically wherever you can.

## A weekly stock routine

Once a week, check the variations closest to their threshold and the ones that sold fastest. Compare them with what is in carts and what you expect from upcoming promotions. Place reorders for anything that will hit its threshold before new stock can arrive, remembering that supplier lead times grow in busy seasons. Ten minutes a week is far cheaper than cancelling orders or apologizing for delays.

## In short

Track stock per variation, set stop-sell thresholds above zero, watch what sits in carts during busy weeks and reorder bestsellers early. A short weekly routine is enough to avoid most stockouts and cancelled orders.

If you only change one thing after reading this, make it the habit you can repeat every week. Small, steady improvements to how you run a custom store add up faster than any single big change, and they are much easier for a small team to keep doing.
