Every print shop has had the logo that looked fine in an email and terrible on a banner. The cause is almost always the difference between vector and raster artwork.

## Vector: drawn with shapes

Vector files describe lines and curves, so they can be scaled to any size and stay sharp. Logos, text and flat illustrations should be vectors whenever you can get them.

## Raster: made of pixels

Photos and most images from the web are raster. They have a fixed number of pixels, so enlarging them makes edges soft or blocky.

## The zoom test

The easiest way to explain this to a customer is to zoom in. If the edges stay crisp at 400 percent, the file is fine. If they turn to squares, ask for a better version before you print.

## Common mistakes

- **Saving a vector as a JPG.** Exporting a logo to JPG turns sharp shapes back into pixels. Keep the original SVG, PDF or EPS.
- **Placing a raster inside a PDF.** A PDF is not automatically vector. If the logo inside it is a photo of a logo, it will still blur.
- **Enlarging a small image.** More pixels do not add detail. The result is soft, however good the software is.

## What to ask customers for

When a customer uploads a logo, ask for the original file from whoever designed it. Designers almost always have a vector version. For photos, ask for the largest version they have, straight from the camera or phone, not one shared through a messaging app, which usually compresses images. A short note on the upload screen explaining this saves many follow-up emails and reprints.

## In short

Ask for vectors for logos and text, the largest original for photos, and use the zoom test when in doubt. A short note on the upload screen explains it to customers and prevents most blurry prints before they happen.
