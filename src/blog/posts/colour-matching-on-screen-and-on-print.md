A customer's brand orange looks perfect in their logo file and a little different on every product. That is not a mistake; it is physics, and it can be managed.

## Different systems

Screens create colour with light. Printers create it with ink on a surface. Some screen colours are simply brighter than any ink can be.

## The product changes the colour

Ink on a white cotton tee looks different from the same ink on a grey polyester bottle. Material, texture and base colour all shift the result.

## Keep brand colours consistent

- Ask your printer about spot colours for exact brand matches
- Print a small swatch before a large run
- Keep a reference sample of approved colours

## Set expectations

A realistic preview on the actual product colour avoids most disappointment before it happens.

## Common mistakes

- **Approving colours on a phone.** Phone screens are bright and vivid. Check important colours on a printed sample.
- **One colour for every product.** The same brand orange can need slightly different settings on cotton, ceramic and metal.
- **No reference.** Without an approved sample, every reorder is a fresh debate about what the colour should look like.

## Talking to customers about colour

Explain early, in plain words, that printed colours can look a little different from the screen, especially very bright ones. Show the preview on the actual product colour, and offer a sample print for large or brand-sensitive orders. Customers rarely mind a small difference they were told about in advance; they mind being surprised. A short note on product pages for brand merchandise prevents most colour complaints.

## In short

Screens and presses will never match perfectly, but brand colours can stay consistent: use spot colours or swatch prints, keep an approved reference sample, and tell customers in plain words what to expect before they order.
