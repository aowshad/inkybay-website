A print area is the box on a product where shoppers can place their design. Getting it right is the difference between files you can print and files you have to fix.

## One side per surface

Add a side for every surface you print: front, back, sleeves, or the wrap of a mug. Each side gets its own print area and its own preview.

## Match your printer's limits

Ask your printer for the maximum print size on each product and draw the print area to match. If shoppers can only place designs inside it, every file arrives at a size you can produce.

## Leave a margin

Keep the print area a little away from seams, collars and handles. A design that touches a seam will rarely print straight.

## Test before launch

Place a design that fills the whole print area and check the preview on every side. If it looks right there, it will look right in production.

## Common mistakes

- **Print areas larger than the printer can print.** The preview looks great and production has to shrink the design. Match the real limits.
- **Forgetting the back.** If you print on the back, add the side. Shoppers cannot design what they cannot see.
- **One area for every size.** Small and large garments have different printable widths. Check the smallest size you sell.

## Before you launch a product

Run through the product as a shopper would. Add text, upload an image, switch sides and change the colour. Then look at the order the way production will: is every design inside the area, at a size you can print, on the side you expect? A ten-minute test like this catches most setup problems. Repeat it whenever you change the product photo or the print area, because a new photo can shift where the area sits on the garment.
