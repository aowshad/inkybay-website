Custom products are easy to underprice. Every order looks a little different, so it is tempting to pick a number that feels right and move on. A few minutes with real costs saves a season of thin margins.

## Start from what one order really costs

List every cost for a single item: the blank product, ink or thread, the minutes it takes to print and pack, packaging, payment fees and shipping you absorb. Add them up before you look at what competitors charge.

- Blank product and its shipping to you
- Print or embroidery cost per side
- Labour for setup, printing and packing
- Packaging and payment fees

## Charge for what costs you more

A design on the back as well as the front costs more to print, so it should cost more to buy. The same goes for premium colours, extra print areas and rush orders. Shoppers accept add-on prices when they see them change as they design.

## Keep it simple for shoppers

Round to clean price points and show the total while they customize. A clear price that updates live builds more trust than a low base price with surprises at checkout.

## Review, then adjust

Check your margins every season. If blanks or shipping went up, adjust once, clearly, rather than nudging prices every week.

## Common pricing mistakes

- **Pricing from competitors first.** Their costs, volumes and suppliers are not yours. Use them as a sanity check after you know your own numbers.
- **Forgetting the failed prints.** Misprints, damaged blanks and returns are part of the cost. Add a small allowance, a few percent, to every item.
- **Free personalization.** A name on the back takes time to check, print and pack. If it is free, it is coming out of your margin.
- **One price for every size.** Larger garments often cost more as blanks. A small step up for XXL and above is normal and expected.

## A quick pricing worksheet

Write down the full cost of one item, add your target margin, then round up to a clean price point. Do the same for each add-on: an extra side, a premium colour, gift wrap. Keep the worksheet next to your product list and update it when a supplier changes their prices. When you launch a new product, start from the worksheet, not from the price of a similar item you already sell.
