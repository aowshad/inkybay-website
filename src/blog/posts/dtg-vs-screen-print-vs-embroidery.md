Most custom apparel stores end up using more than one printing method. Knowing where each one shines helps you price fairly and set the right expectations.

## Direct to garment (DTG)

DTG prints the design straight onto the fabric like an inkjet printer. It handles photos and gradients well and has almost no setup, which makes it ideal for one-off and small orders.

## Screen printing

Screen printing pushes ink through a stencil, one colour at a time. Setup takes longer, but every extra item is cheap. It suits team orders and merch drops with a handful of flat colours.

## Embroidery

Embroidery stitches the design in thread. It feels premium and lasts for years, but small text and fine detail get lost. Keep embroidered designs bold and simple.

## Offer more than one

Letting shoppers pick the method on the same product means one listing serves both the single gift and the 200-piece team order. Give each method its own price and its own limits on colours and size.

## Common mistakes

- **Embroidering tiny text.** Thread cannot form letters smaller than about a quarter of an inch. Ask for a simplified logo or switch to print.
- **Screen printing one-offs.** Setup time makes single items expensive. Send small orders to DTG instead.
- **Ignoring the fabric.** DTG works best on cotton; some polyester blends need a different process. Check with your printer before you list a product.

## How to choose, quickly

Ask three questions about each order. How many items? How many colours? Does it need to feel premium? Under a dozen items with full colour points to DTG. Large runs with two or three flat colours point to screen printing. Workwear, caps and anything that should last for years point to embroidery. Write this down for your team so every quote is consistent, and show the method clearly on the product page so shoppers know what they are buying.
