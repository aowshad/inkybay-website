Personalized gifts sell all year, but the last weeks of the year can bring more orders than the rest of the season combined. Preparing in summer makes the rush manageable.

## Choose and test gift products

Pick the products that sell as gifts: mugs, frames, ornaments, blankets. Order samples, test the print and check delivery times now, not in November.

## Publish order-by dates

Shoppers forgive almost anything except a gift that arrives late. Put clear order-by dates on product pages and in your emails.

## Plan capacity

Estimate how many orders you can produce per day and when you will hit that limit. Decide in advance whether you will pause orders or extend delivery times.

## Templates help shoppers start

Ready-made designs for birthdays, weddings and holidays turn browsers into buyers, because starting from something is easier than starting from nothing.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting promotion too late.** Many gift shoppers browse weeks before they buy. Be visible early.
- **Ignoring shipping cut-offs.** Carriers publish their holiday deadlines. Build your order-by dates from theirs, with a buffer.
- **Launching new products in December.** The rush is the worst time to discover a printing problem. Launch earlier and refine.

## After the season

When the rush is over, look at what sold, what was returned and which questions came up most. Note the products that ran out of stock and the weeks production was stretched. These notes are the start of next year's plan. Thank customers with a short email and invite them to order for birthdays and anniversaries during the year, so a seasonal buyer becomes a regular one.

## In short

Choose and test gift products early, publish order-by dates, plan your capacity and prepare templates for popular occasions. Then review the season while it is fresh, so next year's rush starts from a better plan.
