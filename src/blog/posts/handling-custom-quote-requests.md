Some orders do not fit a price list: 500 embroidered jackets, a mixed order across five products, a rush job. A quote request turns those into conversations instead of lost sales.

## Collect the details up front

A good request includes the design, the product, the quantity and the deadline. When shoppers can send a request straight from the design editor, the design comes attached and you skip the back-and-forth.

## Reply quickly

Speed matters more than polish. A quick reply that asks one clarifying question beats a perfect quote three days later.

## Make the quote easy to accept

- One clear total, with what it includes
- The delivery date you can commit to
- A simple way to say yes

## Follow up once

If there is no reply, follow up once before the quote expires. Then let it go.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking for details one email at a time.** Each round trip loses interest. Ask for everything you need in the first reply.
- **Quoting without the design.** The design decides the method, the colours and the cost. Always see it before you price.
- **Vague totals.** "From $8 per item" invites doubt. Give one total for the exact order.

## Turn quotes into repeat business

Keep a record of every quote: what was asked for, what you offered and whether it was accepted. Patterns appear quickly. If many requests are for the same product in bulk, add quantity pricing so those buyers can order without waiting. If a business orders once, remind them before the same event next year. A well-handled quote is often the start of a long relationship rather than a single order.

## In short

Collect the design, quantity and deadline up front, reply quickly with one clear total, follow up once and keep a record. Handled well, a quote request becomes a repeat customer rather than a single order.
